Best Business Bank Accounts for UK Startups in 2026: Ranked by Real Founder Needs

J
James Okafor
Published October 2026 · 10 min read

Finding the best business bank account for UK startups in 2026 is harder than it looks. The market has shifted — Revolut axed its free tier, challenger banks have matured, and traditional banks are dangling long free periods to win back founders. This guide cuts through the noise and ranks your real options based on what actually matters: cost, eligibility, integrations, and how well each account holds up as your startup grows.

TL;DR — Quick Picks for Time-Pressed Founders

  • Best free account overall: Starling Bank — permanently free, FSCS-protected, and integrates with every major accounting tool.
  • Best free account for sole traders: Mettle (by NatWest) — free forever, and bundles FreeAgent accounting software worth ~£150/year at no cost.
  • Best for international founders or multi-currency: Revolut Business — supports 34 currencies and local GBP/EUR account details, though it now starts at £10/month.

Why Your Bank Account Choice Matters More Than Founders Think

Your business bank account isn't just a place to park money. It determines how fast you can pay suppliers, whether your accounting software syncs automatically, whether you're protected if the bank fails, and — especially for international founders — whether you can open an account at all. Most early-stage founders treat banking as an afterthought and end up switching accounts six months in. This guide is designed to save you that pain.

One thing that's changed significantly heading into 2026: the free business bank account UK landscape has narrowed. Revolut's decision to remove its free tier in January 2026 is a meaningful shake-up. It removes one of the most popular zero-cost options and means you need to scrutinise the remaining free accounts more carefully.

Starling Bank — The Best All-Round Business Bank Account for UK Startups in 2026

Starling consistently tops founder surveys for good reason. It is permanently free — not a 12-month intro period, not a freemium trap — with no monthly fee, no charges on UK transfers, and no limit on transactions. Your deposits are FSCS-protected up to £85,000, which matters if you hold significant runway in your account.

On integrations, Starling connects natively with Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent, and Sage — the four accounting tools that cover the vast majority of UK startups. If you're already thinking about your accounting setup, this out-of-the-box sync is a genuine time-saver.

The catch: Starling requires at least one UK-resident director and does not accept corporate shareholders. If your cap table or director structure is international, you may be rejected at the eligibility stage. For straightforward UK-resident founders building a limited company, though, it is the strongest default choice.

"Starling just works. Transactions import into Xero automatically, support actually responds, and I've never paid a penny in fees." — Common founder sentiment across Reddit, Trustpilot, and startup forums in 2025–26.

Mettle — The Highest-Value Free Account for Sole Traders

Mettle, which is backed by NatWest, doesn't get as much attention as Starling but arguably offers more raw value for sole traders and small limited companies. It is free forever with no transaction charges, which already puts it in a small category. What makes Mettle genuinely exceptional is the bundled FreeAgent accounting software — worth approximately £150 per year — included at no extra cost.

If you were going to pay for FreeAgent anyway (and many UK sole traders do, particularly for Making Tax Digital compliance), Mettle effectively pays for itself before you've made a single transaction. It's also worth noting that Mettle sits within the NatWest Group's infrastructure, so you get the stability of a high-street bank with the UX of a challenger.

The limitation is scale: Mettle is better suited to early-stage sole traders and micro-businesses than to fast-growing startups with complex banking needs. If you're hiring, raising, or operating across multiple currencies, you'll likely outgrow it.

Tide and Monzo — Useful, But Read the Small Print

Both Tide and Monzo Business are popular with UK founders, but both have nuances that catch people out.

Tide has a free tier, but it limits you to 5 free UK transfers per month — every additional transfer costs 20p. For a startup making regular supplier payments, payroll transfers, or VAT submissions, that 20p-per-transfer model adds up fast. If you need volume, Tide's paid plans start at £12.49/month (Plus), with Pro at £27.49 and Max at £69.99. These aren't unreasonable prices, but you should go in with eyes open rather than assuming Tide is a free-forever solution.

Monzo Business Lite is genuinely free and offers FSCS protection up to £120,000 — notably higher than Starling's £85,000 cap, which matters if you're holding significant cash. The free tier is clean and functional. However, if you want invoicing tools, accounting integrations with Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent, or Sage, or the Making Tax Digital compliance feature, you'll need Monzo Business Pro at £9/month. That's a fair price for what you get — just not zero.

The best digital business bank account UK 2026 title is genuinely contested between Starling and Monzo for different founder profiles. Starling wins on zero-cost integrations; Monzo wins on FSCS protection ceiling and a slightly more polished mobile experience.

Revolut Business — Best for International Founders, Now at a Price

Let's address the elephant in the room: Revolut Business removed its free tier in January 2026. The Basic plan now costs £10/month, Grow is £35/month, and Scale is £125/month. This is a significant change and effectively disqualifies Revolut as a zero-cost option for early-stage founders who are watching every pound.

That said, for the right founder profile, Revolut remains the strongest choice. It supports 34 currencies and provides local GBP and EUR account details — meaning overseas customers can pay you as if you were a local business in their country. For SaaS companies billing internationally, ecommerce exporters, or founders with global supplier relationships, this multi-currency infrastructure is hard to match. No other account on this list comes close for international operations.

The Starling vs Tide vs Monzo business account conversation is largely about domestic UK banking. If you're doing meaningful business across borders, Revolut deserves a place in that comparison despite its new monthly fee — the question is whether the currency functionality justifies £10–35/month for your specific situation.

Revolut is also notably more accessible to international founders. Where Starling and Monzo require UK-resident directors and reject non-resident structures, Revolut takes a more flexible approach to eligibility. Wise Business is another option worth considering for this use case — it isn't a full current account but handles multi-currency payments and international transfers extremely well at low cost.

Traditional Banks — When the Intro Period Makes Sense

Don't dismiss high-street banks outright, particularly if you want a relationship manager, branch access, or a business loan down the line. The intro periods are genuinely useful for very early-stage startups:

  • Barclays: Free for 12 months, then £8.50/month
  • Lloyds: Free for 12 months, then £10/month
  • NatWest: Free for 24 months, then standard fees apply
  • Co-operative Bank Directplus: Free for 30 months — the longest intro period available

The Co-op's 30-month free window is genuinely remarkable. If you're a UK-resident founder starting a business bank account for limited company UK purposes and you expect to be pre-revenue or low-revenue for the next two years, 30 months of free banking with a FSCS-protected, full-featured traditional account is hard to argue against on cost grounds alone.

The trade-off is UX. Traditional banks are slower to open (days or weeks vs. minutes for challengers), have less polished apps, and their accounting integrations are patchier. For founders who live in their dashboard, this friction is real.

Eligibility — The Factor Most Comparison Sites Ignore

Here is the honest truth that most bank account comparison articles gloss over: eligibility requirements will disqualify you from some accounts before you even look at features.

The rules vary significantly:

  • Starling Bank: Requires at least one UK-resident director. Rejects companies with corporate shareholders or non-resident directors.
  • Monzo Business: Similar UK-residency requirements for directors.
  • Tide: Generally more flexible on company structure, but still requires UK-registered companies.
  • Revolut Business: More accessible to international founders and non-resident directors, making it the go-to for cross-border startups.
  • Wise Business: The most internationally friendly option for non-resident founders — not a full current account but excellent for payment and currency management.

If you're a UK business bank account for non-resident founders situation — perhaps you've incorporated in the UK but are based elsewhere — Revolut Business or Wise Business should be your starting point, not Starling or Monzo.

Also check: some accounts require your company to be registered for a certain period, have a UK-registered address, or pass automated risk checks. If you've just incorporated, start the application process early — some approvals take longer than founders expect.

How to Choose: A Practical Framework

Rather than prescribing a single winner, here's how to think through the decision for your specific situation:

  • UK-resident founder, limited company, want free forever: Start with Starling. If you're a sole trader, check Mettle for the bundled FreeAgent value.
  • UK-resident founder, want the longest free period before fees kick in: Co-operative Bank Directplus (30 months) or NatWest (24 months).
  • International founder or multi-currency needs: Revolut Business is your primary option. Wise Business as a complement for payments.
  • Free account with highest FSCS protection: Monzo Business Lite at £120,000.
  • Need accounting software bundled in: Mettle (FreeAgent included) or Monzo Pro (£9/month with Xero/QuickBooks/FreeAgent/Sage integrations).
  • Expect high transaction volume on a free account: Avoid Tide's free tier. Starling has no transfer limits.

Whichever account you choose, make sure it connects cleanly to your accounting and payments stack. A bank account that saves you £10/month in fees but costs you two hours a week in manual reconciliation is a false economy.
