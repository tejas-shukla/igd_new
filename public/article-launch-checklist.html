The Complete Founder's Checklist: 47 Steps to Launch Day

A
Sarah Chen
Updated April 2026 · 12 min read

Launching a business can feel like staring at a mountain with no trail. The truth is, almost every successful launch follows the same broad sequence — and once you can see the whole path, the climb gets a lot less intimidating. This is the condensed version of the 47-step checklist we use inside IGotDomain, grouped into the five phases every founder moves through.

Phase 1 — Validate the Idea (Days 1–3)

Before you spend a penny, prove that someone actually wants what you're planning to build. Validation isn't about certainty; it's about reducing risk cheaply.

  • Write a one-sentence description of the problem you solve and who has it.
  • Talk to ten people who fit your target customer — listen more than you pitch.
  • Search for existing competitors. Competition is a signal of demand, not a reason to quit.
  • Put up a simple landing page and measure whether people will leave an email.
If you can't get ten strangers mildly interested, ten thousand won't magically appear after launch.

Phase 2 — Lock In Your Identity (Days 3–7)

With demand confirmed, claim your name and digital footprint. This is where a good domain matters.

  • Brainstorm 15–20 name candidates; keep them short and easy to spell.
  • Check domain availability and compare prices across registrars — first-year deals can hide steep renewals.
  • Secure matching social handles before you commit.
  • Sketch a simple logo and pick two brand colors. Done beats perfect.

Phase 3 — Build Your Home Base (Week 2)

Your website is your storefront, your pitch and your credibility in one. Pick the builder that matches your model rather than the one with the loudest ads — an online store has very different needs from a content site.

Match the tool to the job

  • Selling products? A commerce-first platform pays for itself.
  • Publishing content? A flexible CMS will serve you for years.
  • Showcasing work? A design-led builder gets you live fastest.

Phase 4 — Set Up the Money (Week 2–3)

Founders consistently underestimate how much cleaner life is when business finances are separated from day one.

  • Open a dedicated business account before your first sale.
  • Choose accounting software early so every transaction is categorized as it happens.
  • Understand your basic tax obligations for your country and structure.

Phase 5 — Prepare to Launch (Week 3–4)

Momentum on launch day comes from work you do before it.

  • Build a small waitlist and warm it up with updates.
  • Prepare launch-day assets: announcement post, email, and a simple demo.
  • Line up a handful of friendly early users to give feedback fast.
  • Pick one channel to go deep on rather than five you'll do badly.
