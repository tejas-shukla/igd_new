Xero vs Wave Accounting Software for Small Business (2026): The Decision Has Changed

J
James Okafor
Published October 2026 · 9 min read

The Xero vs Wave accounting software small business debate looked very different a year ago. Wave was the clear default answer for bootstrapped founders who needed professional invoicing and basic bookkeeping without opening their wallets. That calculus shifted materially on June 1, 2026, when Wave quietly restructured its free tier — and thousands of small business owners woke up to find their workflows broken. This article breaks down exactly what changed, who each tool still serves well, and how to make the right call for your specific situation in 2026.

TL;DR — The Short Version

  • Wave's free plan changed fundamentally in June 2026 — automatic bank feeds, accountant access, and receipt scanning are now behind a $16–$19/month paywall, making the free "Starter" tier impractical for most real businesses.
  • Xero at $55/month (Growing plan) is the stronger all-around pick for small businesses with a team, growth plans, or more than a handful of monthly transactions.
  • Wave Starter still makes sense for one-person freelance operations with very low transaction volume — but understand its new limits before you commit.

What Actually Changed With the Wave Accounting Free Plan in 2026

The Wave accounting free plan 2026 restructure is the single biggest reason this comparison needs revisiting. On June 1, 2026, Wave moved three previously free features — automatic bank feeds, collaborator and accountant access, and receipt scanning — exclusively to its new Pro tier, priced at $16 to $19 per month depending on billing cycle. Existing free-plan users were automatically migrated to the restricted "Starter" plan with no opt-in required.

Wave Starter (free) still includes:

  • Unlimited invoicing and estimates
  • Manual income and expense tracking
  • Core financial reports (P&L, balance sheet)

What it no longer includes is the feature that made Wave genuinely usable as a daily bookkeeping tool: automatic bank feeds. Without bank feeds, every transaction must be entered by hand. For a freelancer sending five invoices a month, that's manageable. For any business running 30, 50, or 100+ monthly transactions, manual entry isn't bookkeeping — it's a part-time job. The Wave accounting bank feeds paywall is the critical detail most comparison articles written before June 2026 miss entirely.

"We didn't realize the bank connection was gone until our accountant tried to log in and couldn't. Both problems hit us at the same time." — Common founder experience post-migration

Xero Pricing Plans 2026 — What You Actually Get at Each Tier

Understanding Xero pricing plans 2026 requires looking past the headline number to what each tier actually unlocks. Xero currently offers three US tiers:

  • Early — $25/month: Capped at 20 invoices and 5 bills per month. Automatic bank feeds, reconciliation, and unlimited users included. Good for very early-stage businesses with low document volume — but the invoice cap will surprise you faster than you expect.
  • Growing — $55/month: Unlimited invoices, bills, and transactions. This is the plan most small businesses should evaluate. Still includes unlimited users, which matters the moment you have a business partner, a VA, or a bookkeeper who needs access.
  • Established — $90/month: Adds multicurrency, project tracking, expense claims, and advanced analytics. Built for businesses operating internationally or managing project-based profitability.

The unlimited-users structure deserves more attention than it gets. Most competing accounting platforms charge per seat. Xero's flat fee means your accountant, your co-founder, and your operations manager can all have full access without the cost scaling upward. For a two-person founding team, that's immediately meaningful.

Wave Pro vs Xero Comparison — Feature by Feature

Running a proper Wave Pro vs Xero comparison means looking at the version of Wave that most growing businesses will actually need — the $16–$19/month Pro plan — not the stripped Starter tier. Here's how they stack up on the features that matter most:

  • Bank feeds: Both Wave Pro and all Xero plans include automatic bank feeds. Xero's feed connections are generally considered more stable across a wider range of financial institutions.
  • Invoicing: Wave Pro has no invoice cap and is genuinely strong here — customizable templates, automatic payment reminders, and online payment acceptance. Xero Early is capped at 20/month; Xero Growing is unlimited.
  • Multi-user access: Xero includes unlimited users on every plan. Wave Pro supports collaborator and accountant access, but is less granular on permission levels.
  • Integrations: Xero connects with over 1,000 third-party tools — payroll platforms, CRMs, e-commerce systems, inventory tools. Wave offers a much smaller integration ecosystem, and native inventory management and project tracking are entirely absent on any Wave plan.
  • Inventory and project tracking: Available on Xero Established. Not available on Wave at any tier.
  • Geographic reach: Xero supports multicurrency and is built for businesses operating internationally. Wave's core features are limited to the US and Canada — a hard wall if you invoice in multiple currencies or have international suppliers.
  • Mobile receipt capture: Available on Wave Pro. Available on Xero Established.

The Hidden Cost in Wave's Payments Model

This is the number most founders skip past — and it's the one that can make the entire software cost conversation irrelevant. Wave's payment processing fee is 2.9% + $0.60 per transaction on all plans, including Pro. That's a market-rate fee, comparable to Stripe and Square, so it's not a penalty — but it matters when you're comparing "free" Wave to paid Xero.

Run the math on a business processing $10,000/month in card revenue:

  • 2.9% × $10,000 = $290 in percentage fees
  • $0.60 × 50 transactions (estimate) = $30 in per-transaction fees
  • Monthly total: ~$320 | Annual total: ~$3,480/year

That processing cost alone dwarfs the annual subscription price of Xero Growing ($660/year) by more than five times. The lesson: if you're choosing Wave primarily to save on software costs while running meaningful card revenue through it, the math doesn't hold up the way it appears to. Compare your actual total cost of ownership, not just the subscription line.

Best Accounting Software for Small Business 2026 — Who Should Choose What

Finding the best accounting software for small business 2026 isn't about picking a universal winner — it's about matching the tool to the business stage. Here's how we'd frame it:

Wave Starter (free) is right for you if:

  • You're a solo freelancer or sole proprietor with fewer than a dozen monthly transactions
  • You're comfortable with manual transaction entry and have time to do it consistently
  • You don't need your accountant to have direct platform access
  • You're in the US or Canada and have no international invoicing needs

Wave Pro ($16–$19/month) is right for you if:

  • You want Wave's invoicing experience but need bank feeds and accountant access
  • Your transaction volume is moderate and your business is straightforward
  • You're not planning significant growth in the near term that would demand deeper integrations

Xero Growing ($55/month) is right for you if:

  • You have a co-founder, bookkeeper, or accountant who needs access — unlimited users is a real cost advantage
  • You're integrated into or planning to integrate with other tools (e-commerce, payroll, CRM)
  • You want your accounting software to scale with the business rather than requiring a platform switch at 20 employees
  • You operate internationally or invoice in multiple currencies

Xero Established ($90/month) is right for you if:

  • You're managing projects and need profitability tracking at the project level
  • You have multi-currency exposure
  • You want advanced analytics baked in rather than bolted on

Free Accounting Software for Freelancers 2026 — Is Wave Still Worth It?

The answer to whether free accounting software for freelancers 2026 exists in any practical sense is: barely, and only for a specific type of freelancer. Wave Starter remains legitimately free and legitimately useful if your monthly bookkeeping amounts to recording a handful of invoices, manually logging a few expenses, and pulling a quarterly P&L. That describes a meaningful number of part-time freelancers and early-stage solopreneurs.

But if your freelance business has grown to the point where you're reconciling bank statements, sharing access with an accountant at tax time, or snapping photos of receipts on the go — those workflows now require Wave Pro at minimum. At $16–$19/month, Wave Pro is still a reasonable value, but the psychological shift matters: Wave is no longer a free tool with optional paid upgrades. It's a paid tool with a very limited free entry point.

G2 ratings as of mid-2026 reflect that Wave still satisfies its core users: Wave holds a 4.4/5 from 400+ reviews versus Xero's 4.3/5 from 600+ reviews. The gap is negligible, and Wave's slightly higher score suggests its remaining user base — the freelancers and very small businesses it's actually built for — are genuinely happy with it. The risk is assuming that target includes you when it may not anymore.

What to Check Before You Decide

Before committing to either platform, run through this short checklist. Your answers will clarify the decision faster than any feature matrix:

  • How many transactions per month? Under 20 and you can manually enter them: Wave Starter works. Over 30: you need bank feeds, which means Wave Pro or Xero.
  • How many people need accounting access? If it's just you: cost is similar. If it's you plus an accountant plus a bookkeeper: Xero's unlimited-user model saves real money.
  • Do you need integrations? Shopify, Gusto, HubSpot, or any operational tool: Xero's 1,000+ integration library is where you want to be.
  • Do you invoice internationally or in multiple currencies? Wave is a hard no. Xero Established handles this natively.
  • What's your actual payment processing volume? Run the 2.9% + $0.60 math on your real numbers before deciding Wave's lower software cost is a savings.
