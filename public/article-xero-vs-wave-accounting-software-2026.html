Xero vs Wave Accounting Software (2026): Which Is Right for Your Small Business?

J
James Okafor
Published June 2026 · 9 min read

Choosing between Xero vs Wave accounting software for your small business is one of those decisions that looks simple on the surface — one is free, one isn't — but quickly gets complicated when you dig into the real costs, limitations, and growth ceilings. This guide cuts through the noise so you can make the right call for where your business is right now, and where it's heading.

TL;DR — Xero vs Wave at a Glance

  • Wave is genuinely free for core accounting — invoicing, expense tracking, and reporting — making it a strong starting point for freelancers, solopreneurs, and early-stage startups watching every dollar.
  • Xero costs more but does significantly more — 1,000+ app integrations, deeper reporting, and a more scalable feature set justify the price for businesses with growing complexity.
  • The real cost of Wave adds up fast — payment processing fees, payroll add-ons, and limited support mean "free" isn't always free once you're transacting at volume.

Wave Accounting Pricing 2026: What Free Actually Costs You

Wave's pitch is hard to argue with at first glance. The Starter plan is permanently free and covers unlimited invoicing, expense tracking, and core double-entry accounting. That's real value, not a 14-day trial. For many solo founders, it's genuinely all they need.

The Pro plan at $16/month adds automatic bank imports, receipt scanning, and priority support — a reasonable upgrade if manual data entry is eating your time.

But here's where the Wave accounting pricing for 2026 gets more nuanced. The "free" label starts to blur once you factor in:

  • Credit card processing: 2.9% + $0.60 per transaction (3.4% + $0.60 for Amex). These are standard market rates, but they're not free.
  • ACH/bank transfer fees: 1% per transaction with a $1 minimum — a fee that many competitors either absorb or waive.
  • Payroll: Starts at $40/month plus $6 per employee. Add two employees and you're at $52/month before you've paid for anything else.
  • Support: Free plan users get chatbot support only. If something breaks at month-end close, you're on your own.

If you're processing $10,000/month through Wave Payments, you're paying around $350 in transaction fees alone. That math changes the conversation significantly.

Xero Pricing Plans for Small Business: What You Get at Each Tier

Xero has no free plan — full stop. But its Xero pricing plans for small business are tiered in a way that tries to match cost to need:

  • Early — $25/month: Covers basic invoicing, bank reconciliation, and bill management. The catch: you're capped at 20 invoices and 5 bills per month. For a growing business, that ceiling arrives fast.
  • Growing — $55/month: Removes the invoice and bill caps entirely, adding unlimited invoicing and bulk transaction reconciliation. This is where Xero starts making real sense for active businesses.
  • Established — $90/month: Adds multi-currency support, advanced analytics, expense claims, and project tracking. Built for businesses with operational complexity.

One honest frustration worth flagging: Xero has raised prices without always delivering proportional feature upgrades, and users on review platforms have noticed. If you're on the Early plan and hitting invoice caps regularly, you're being nudged toward $55/month whether you're ready for it or not.

Bottom line on pricing: Wave wins on entry cost. Xero wins on value-per-feature once your business outgrows the basics.

Wave vs Xero Features Comparison: Where Each Tool Actually Shines

Looking at a Wave vs Xero features comparison side by side, the gap is wider than the price difference suggests.

Where Wave holds its own:

  • Unlimited invoicing on all plans (including free) — this genuinely beats Xero's Early plan cap of 20 invoices per month
  • Clean, beginner-friendly interface that non-accountants can navigate confidently
  • Built-in payments, invoicing, and accounting in one place with no learning curve
  • Solid G2 rating of 4.4/5 across 400+ reviews as of March 2026

Where Xero pulls ahead:

  • Native integrations with 1,000+ third-party apps — versus Wave's native connections limited to Shopify, PayPal, Etsy, and Zapier
  • More robust reporting and audit trails that hold up to accountant scrutiny
  • Multi-currency invoicing (on the Established plan at $90/month)
  • SelectHub analyst rating of 84 vs Wave's 65, with Xero meeting 86.3% of typical small business requirements compared to Wave's 51.8%
  • Xero holds a 4.3/5 on G2 across 600+ reviews — slightly lower than Wave in user sentiment, but from a larger reviewer base with more enterprise-level users in the mix

That integration gap is significant if you're building a connected operational stack. Wave's Zapier connection helps bridge some gaps, but it's not the same as native integrations with tools like HubSpot, Stripe, Gusto, or Shopify POS. If your payments setup involves multiple processors or your website feeds directly into your accounting, Xero's ecosystem becomes a genuine competitive advantage.

Accounting Software for Freelancers and Startups: Who Should Use Which

When it comes to accounting software for freelancers and startups, the right answer depends less on features and more on where you are in your business journey.

Wave is the right call if you are:

  • A freelancer or solopreneur with straightforward income and expenses
  • A new founder pre-revenue or in early revenue stage who needs zero cash outlay on software
  • A micro-business with high invoice volume but low transaction complexity — Wave's unlimited invoicing on its free tier genuinely beats Xero Early here
  • Someone operating entirely in one currency with no plans to go international
  • Comfortable with self-serve, chatbot-level support when issues arise

Xero is the right call if you are:

  • A startup with multiple team members, expense claims, or payroll complexity
  • A business that relies on a connected tool stack — CRM, inventory, payroll, ecommerce — and needs those integrations to work reliably
  • A founder whose accountant or bookkeeper already uses Xero (a very common scenario)
  • A business processing transactions in multiple currencies
  • Someone who values real human support when things go wrong at quarter-end

It's also worth thinking about your business banking setup. Xero's direct bank feed integrations are broader and more reliable across more institutions, which can save meaningful time in reconciliation each month.

Multi-Currency and International Business: A Clear Winner

If you invoice clients or pay suppliers in foreign currencies, this section matters. Wave does not support multi-currency — not on free, not on Pro. That's a hard limit, not a workaround situation.

Xero supports multi-currency invoicing, but only on its most expensive Established plan at $90/month. That's a significant price jump from the $55 Growing plan for what is, in 2026, a fairly standard business need.

If international transactions are part of your current or near-future business model, Xero is the only realistic choice between these two. But also worth asking: at $90/month, are there other tools in the market worth comparing? That's exactly the kind of question worth running through a proper tool comparison before committing.

When to Upgrade from Wave to Xero: The Real Signals

Knowing when to upgrade from Wave to Xero isn't just about revenue — it's about the friction points that start costing you time and money. Here are the clearest signals it's time to make the switch:

  • Your accountant keeps asking for exports. If your accountant or bookkeeper is on Xero, staying on Wave creates manual work every reporting period.
  • You're hitting integration walls. When your payment processor, inventory tool, or email marketing platform doesn't connect natively with Wave, you're either doing double data entry or rigging up Zapier workarounds that break at the worst moments.
  • You've hired employees. Wave's payroll starts at $40/month plus $6 per employee. Once you're paying for payroll, the price gap between Wave and Xero Growing narrows considerably — and Xero gives you significantly more in return.
  • Your payment processing fees are eating margin. If you're pushing high volume through Wave Payments, those transaction fees compound fast. At that point, re-evaluating your entire payments setup makes sense.
  • You need a real audit trail. As you raise money, bring on partners, or prepare for any kind of due diligence, the quality and depth of your financial records matters. Xero's reporting holds up better under scrutiny.
  • Support matters to you. Free Wave users get chatbot-only support. If that's felt like a problem even once, it's a sign you've outgrown the free tier.

The Honest Verdict: Xero vs Wave for Your Small Business

Here's the straightforward take after looking at all the data: Wave is one of the best free accounting tools available, and for a large segment of small businesses — solo operators, early-stage founders, simple service businesses — it's genuinely sufficient and the financially smart choice.

But "free" has a ceiling, and Wave hits it sooner than most people expect. Limited integrations, no multi-currency, chatbot-only support, and real transaction costs on payments mean that as your business scales, Wave increasingly costs you in hidden ways — time, friction, and opportunity.

Xero costs more upfront but scales with you. Its 1,000+ integrations, stronger reporting, and accountant-friendly workflows make it the more durable long-term choice for businesses with ambition beyond the basics. The Early plan's invoice cap is a genuine frustration, so if you're going Xero, plan your budget around the Growing plan at $55/month from the start.

The decision isn't really "which is better" — it's "which is right for where I am today and where I'll be in 12 months." Start with Wave if budget is tight and your needs are simple. Move to Xero when the friction costs more than the subscription.

If you're also sorting out your business bank account, domain, or website platform at the same time, it's worth looking at how your accounting tool connects across your whole stack before locking in.
