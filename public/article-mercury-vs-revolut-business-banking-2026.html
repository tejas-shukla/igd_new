Mercury vs Revolut Business Banking (2026): Which Is Better for Founders?

J
James Okafor
Published July 2026 · 9 min read

Choosing between Mercury vs Revolut business banking is one of the first real decisions a founder faces — and getting it wrong can mean frozen funds, surprise fees, or a banking setup that doesn't scale with you. Both are neobanks built for modern businesses, but they serve genuinely different founders. This breakdown covers everything you need to make the right call in 2026.

TL;DR — Mercury vs Revolut Business at a Glance

  • Mercury wins for US-focused VC-backed startups: $0 base tier, $5M FDIC coverage, and a dominant position in the Y Combinator ecosystem make it the default for pre-seed through Series B tech companies.
  • Revolut wins for globally-operating businesses: 25+ currencies at near-interbank rates and multi-currency accounts give it a clear edge if you're paying international vendors or running a cross-border team.
  • Both carry real risks: abrupt account closures, compliance freezes, and limited customer support are documented problems on both platforms — neither should be your only bank account.

Pricing Plans: What You Actually Pay in 2026

The best business bank account for startups in 2026 shouldn't drain your runway before you've earned your first dollar — so let's start with cost.

Mercury keeps its base tier genuinely free: $0 monthly fees, no minimum balance requirement, free ACH transfers, and free domestic USD wires. That's not a promotional rate — it's the standard offering. If you need more, paid plans step up to Mercury Plus at $35/month (recurring invoices, priority support, higher transfer limits) and Mercury Pro at $350/month (advanced NetSuite accounting automations, dedicated relationship managers, and custom API access). Most early-stage founders will never need to leave the free tier.

Revolut Business US pricing runs four tiers as of April 2026:

  • Basic — $0/month
  • Grow — $30/month
  • Scale — $180/month
  • Enterprise — custom pricing

Annual billing saves you 22–28% across paid tiers. The catch is that the free Basic plan is genuinely limited — you'll hit FX allowance caps and team member restrictions quickly if you're doing real volume. Most growing teams land on Grow or Scale, which pushes the cost comparison closer to Mercury Plus/Pro territory.

Bottom line on pricing: Mercury's free tier is more generous. Revolut's paid tiers offer more international functionality per dollar if your business needs it.

Mercury Bank Review: Built for VC-Backed Tech Startups

A proper Mercury bank review for founders has to start with one number: 200,000+ customers, including over 40% of the most recent Y Combinator batch. Mercury didn't earn that by accident — it built a product that matches exactly how US tech startups operate.

The core banking product is clean and fast. Founders get a USD checking and savings account, a free debit card, virtual cards for team spending, and an intuitive dashboard that doesn't require a finance degree to navigate. ACH and domestic wires are free. Bill pay and basic invoicing are included at the free tier.

Where Mercury gets interesting for growing startups is its financial stack beyond basic banking:

  • Mercury Treasury — for accounts holding $250K+, idle cash is automatically invested in government-backed securities managed by J.P. Morgan Asset Management and Morgan Stanley, currently yielding up to 3.85% APY. If you've just closed a seed round, this is meaningful money.
  • Working capital loans — available for e-commerce brands with $250K+ in annual revenue.
  • Venture debt — structured financing for high-growth startups that need non-dilutive capital.

In December 2025, Mercury applied for an OCC national bank charter, and in April 2026 received conditional OCC approval — a landmark moment that will eventually bring proprietary payment infrastructure and potential Zelle integration. This matters for long-term stability and independence from partner banks.

Revolut Business Pricing Plans 2026: Built for Global Teams

Understanding Revolut Business pricing plans in 2026 means understanding what you're actually buying: a multi-currency operating account designed for businesses that move money across borders regularly.

Even on the free Basic plan, Revolut gives you multi-currency accounts in 25+ currencies, local IBANs in select markets, and the ability to hold, exchange, and send money internationally. The plan tiers mainly govern how much of that you can do before fees kick in:

  • Basic: $1,000/month in fee-free FX exchanges
  • Grow: $20,000/month in fee-free FX exchanges
  • Scale: $80,000/month in fee-free FX exchanges

Beyond those allowances, Revolut charges a 0.6% markup on FX conversions — and adds a 1% weekend surcharge when interbank markets are closed. For context, Mercury charges a flat 1% on international local-currency vendor payments and up to 3% on non-USD card transactions regardless of volume. If you're doing $30K/month in international transfers, the math strongly favors Revolut on Grow ($30/month) over Mercury at any tier.

Mercury vs Revolut International Payments: The Clearest Differentiator

When it comes to Mercury vs Revolut international payments, there's no contest — Revolut wins for any business operating beyond US borders.

Mercury is, at its core, a USD-first bank. It handles domestic US payments beautifully but was not designed for international complexity. Its 1% FX fee on vendor payments and up to 3% on card transactions in non-USD currencies adds up fast for businesses paying global contractors, running international ad spend, or managing a distributed team in Europe or Asia.

Revolut, by contrast, was built from day one around currency conversion. You can hold 25+ currencies simultaneously, pay international vendors at rates close to the interbank (mid-market) rate within your plan's allowance, and issue multi-currency cards to team members. For a startup with a US entity but a globally distributed operation, Revolut's infrastructure is genuinely superior.

If more than 20% of your monthly spending is in non-USD currencies, Revolut will save you real money. If 95%+ of your spend is USD-denominated, Mercury's free tier is hard to beat.

FDIC Insurance and Safety: A Critical Gap You Can't Ignore

This is where the neobank for tech startups comparison gets serious — especially for founders who've just raised a round and are holding significant cash.

Mercury provides up to $5 million in FDIC insurance through a 20-bank sweep network. That's 20x the standard $250K federal limit. For a startup that just closed a $2M seed round and needs to park that capital safely, this is a material advantage over most banking alternatives.

Revolut Business in the US currently has no FDIC insurance. It operates through partner Lead Bank, which means your deposits may have some pass-through protection, but Revolut itself is not an FDIC-insured institution. Revolut filed for its own OCC national bank charter in March 2026, but approval is expected to take 12–18 months from that date. Until that charter is granted, the insurance gap is real.

For VC-backed startups holding $500K+ in operating cash, this difference alone may settle the debate. The FDIC coverage Mercury provides isn't a minor footnote — it's the reason so many Y Combinator founders default to Mercury after closing a round.

Mercury Bank vs Revolut Multi-Currency: Who Should Use Each

The Mercury bank vs Revolut multi-currency question ultimately comes down to your business model, not which product is objectively better.

Mercury is the right choice if you are:

  • A US-incorporated startup (Delaware C-Corp or LLC) with primarily USD revenue and expenses
  • Raising or planning to raise VC funding — Mercury's ecosystem integrations with investors, cap table tools, and accountants is unmatched
  • Holding significant cash reserves post-fundraise and need FDIC coverage above $250K
  • Running a SaaS, tech, or e-commerce business primarily serving US customers
  • A solo founder or small team that doesn't need complex multi-entity structures

Revolut Business is the right choice if you are:

  • Operating across multiple countries or currencies regularly
  • Paying international contractors, suppliers, or employees in their local currencies
  • Running a business with significant non-USD expenses — marketing in Europe, contractors in South America, suppliers in Asia
  • A bootstrapped or revenue-funded business that doesn't need to park large cash reserves
  • Managing a team across borders who need their own business cards in local currencies

Some founders use both — Mercury as their primary USD operating account for investor wires and US payroll, and Revolut for international vendor payments and multi-currency card spend. It's not a crazy setup if your business genuinely spans both worlds.

The Risks Nobody Talks About (But Should)

Any honest comparison has to address the documented downsides of both platforms, because these aren't edge cases — they're patterns.

Mercury account closures: Mercury has a documented history of abruptly closing accounts without prior notice or explanation. This appears to be tied to compliance reviews triggered by transaction patterns, international activity, or business model flags. When an account is closed, funds can be inaccessible for weeks while the review process plays out. Mercury offers no weekend customer support, which compounds the problem if an issue surfaces on a Friday afternoon.

Revolut Business account freezes: Revolut users frequently report accounts being frozen during compliance reviews — sometimes for days or weeks. Like Mercury, Revolut offers no phone support at all, meaning disputes and appeals must be resolved through in-app chat or email, which can be slow and frustrating when you're trying to make payroll.

The practical implication for founders: neither Mercury nor Revolut should be your only business bank account. Maintain a backup relationship — even a basic free account at a traditional bank — so that if your neobank account is frozen or closed, you can continue operating without missing payments or payroll.

This isn't fear-mongering. It's the same advice you'd hear from any founder who's been through it. Setting up a backup account takes 30 minutes and costs nothing. Not having one when you need it can cost you weeks of stress and missed obligations.

The Bottom Line: Which One Should You Choose?

After laying out the full picture, the answer for most founders is relatively clear:

Choose Mercury if you're a US-focused startup, especially VC-backed, that needs a clean free banking experience, strong FDIC protection on large cash balances, and a product that integrates natively with the startup ecosystem. The Treasury product alone makes it compelling if you're holding post-round cash. Mercury's pending national bank charter also signals long-term stability.

Choose Revolut Business if your business is genuinely global — paying vendors, contractors, or team members in multiple currencies regularly. The multi-currency accounts and near-interbank FX rates on paid plans will save you more than enough to justify the monthly fee.

For everything else your business needs — from setting up your domain and website to choosing the right accounting software, payments processor, or business email — the tools you pair with your bank matter just as much as the bank itself.
