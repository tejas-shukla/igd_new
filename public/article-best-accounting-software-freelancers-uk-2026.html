Best Accounting Software for Freelancers UK 2026: Your MTD-Ready Guide

J
James Okafor
Published September 2026 · 10 min read

Finding the best accounting software for freelancers UK got a lot more urgent in 2026. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD for ITSA) became mandatory from 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords earning over £50,000 — and the threshold drops to £30,000 in April 2027, then £20,000 in April 2028. That means MTD-compatible software is no longer a nice-to-have. It's a legal requirement that's coming for most freelancers faster than they think. This guide cuts through the noise and tells you exactly which tool fits your situation, your budget, and your future plans.

TL;DR — The Quick Verdict

  • Best all-rounder: FreeAgent — built for UK sole traders, handles MTD, Self Assessment, and VAT from one dashboard, and it's free if you bank with NatWest, Mettle, or Tide.
  • Best budget pick: Pandle — genuinely free tier with invoicing and bank feeds; upgrade to Pandle Pro for just £5/month to unlock MTD filing.
  • Best for growth or international clients: Xero — strong multi-currency support, accountant-friendly, and a clear path as your business scales.

Why Making Tax Digital Software for Self-Employed UK Freelancers Can't Wait

If you're a sole trader earning over £50,000, you're already in scope for MTD for ITSA as of April 2026. You now need to submit quarterly digital updates to HMRC using Making Tax Digital software for self-employed UK taxpayers — a traditional spreadsheet or paper records won't cut it. The earning threshold drops to £30,000 in April 2027 and then to £20,000 in April 2028, which means the majority of full-time freelancers in the UK will need to be compliant within the next two years. Getting your software in place now — rather than scrambling closer to your deadline — gives you time to learn the system, clean up your records, and avoid penalties. Every tool on this list is MTD-compatible, but they vary significantly in how painlessly they guide you through the process.

FreeAgent vs Xero for Sole Traders — Which All-Rounder Wins?

This is the comparison most freelancers land on eventually, so let's settle it. FreeAgent vs Xero for sole traders comes down to one key question: are you primarily running a UK-focused freelance business, or are you growing something bigger?

FreeAgent was built in Edinburgh specifically around the UK tax system. That heritage shows. Self Assessment, VAT returns, MTD submissions, and Corporation Tax are all handled from a single dashboard without needing workarounds or third-party add-ons. Paid plans start at £16.50/month for the first six months (ex. VAT), and — crucially — FreeAgent is completely free if you bank with NatWest, Mettle, or Tide. For most sole traders, that combination of UK-specific depth and potential zero cost makes it the default winner.

Xero starts around £20/month and earns its place when you have international clients (its multi-currency support is genuinely excellent), when you work closely with an accountant who prefers the Xero ecosystem, or when you're planning to take on staff or grow into a limited company. Be aware: Xero's cheapest plan caps you at 20 invoices per month, and Self Assessment support is notably less intuitive than FreeAgent's. If you're billing more than 20 clients or want a frictionless tax experience, you'll likely need a higher Xero tier — and the maths quickly favours FreeAgent.

Bottom line: If you're a UK-based sole trader who wants tax sorted without thinking about it — FreeAgent. If you're scaling, international, or accountant-led — Xero.

Free Accounting Software UK Freelancers Can Actually Use in 2026

The free tier landscape shifted significantly this year. Wave — which was the go-to free accounting software UK freelancers relied on — has exited the UK market entirely. That's pushed a lot of people toward FreeAgent (free via banking partners) and Xero, but there are two genuinely free options still worth knowing about.

Pandle is the standout. Its free tier covers invoicing, bank feeds, and basic bookkeeping — more than enough for a freelancer just starting out or keeping things simple. When you need MTD filing and receipt uploads, Pandle Pro costs just £5 + VAT/month. That's an exceptionally low price of entry for full MTD compliance.

Sage UK also offers a free plan for sole traders with MTD compatibility baked in. Sage is investing heavily in AI through its Sage Copilot feature, which is making the platform feel more modern than its reputation suggests. If you're not ready to pay for software yet but need MTD compliance, Sage's free tier is a credible option most freelancers overlook.

QuickBooks Self-Employed UK Review — Best Value for Basic Needs

In this QuickBooks Self-Employed UK review, the headline is simple: it's the most affordable paid entry point at around £8/month (and frequently discounted further, sometimes to £4/month in promotional periods). For a sole trader who wants basic tax estimates, mileage tracking, and expense management without paying for features they'll never use, QuickBooks Self-Employed hits the mark.

Where it falls short: it's less deeply integrated with the UK Self Assessment process than FreeAgent, and it doesn't position itself as a full MTD filing solution in the same way. It's best suited to freelancers with relatively simple finances — regular invoices, some expenses, occasional travel — who want software that makes quarterly estimates easier without a steep learning curve. If your needs grow, you'll likely outgrow it and move to QuickBooks' standard plans (which are priced higher) or switch to a different platform entirely.

Cheapest Accounting Software UK Sole Trader Options Compared

Here's the honest breakdown on the cheapest accounting software UK sole trader options in 2026:

  • Pandle Free — £0/month. Invoicing, bank feeds, basic bookkeeping. Upgrade to Pandle Pro (£5 + VAT/month) for MTD and receipts.
  • Sage Free Plan — £0/month. MTD-compatible, AI features via Sage Copilot. Good option if you're comfortable with Sage's interface.
  • FreeAgent via NatWest/Mettle/Tide — £0/month. Full-featured. The best free option if you're willing to use a compatible business bank account.
  • QuickBooks Self-Employed — ~£8/month. Best paid entry point for sole traders who want tax estimates and expense tracking without complexity.
  • FreeAgent paid — £16.50/month for the first six months (ex. VAT). Worth it if you can't access the free banking route.
  • Xero Starter — ~£20/month. 20-invoice cap. Justified if you need multi-currency or accountant collaboration.

If cost is your primary concern and you're earning under the MTD threshold for now, Pandle Free or the FreeAgent/banking bundle gives you the most value. If you're already in scope for MTD, don't skimp — the cost of getting it wrong is higher than any monthly subscription.

AI-First Bookkeeping — The New Wave Worth Watching

A genuinely new category is emerging for freelancers who find even "simple" accounting software feels like a chore. Tools like Accounted — which uses a WhatsApp-based AI bookkeeper called Penny — let you snap a receipt, send it via WhatsApp, and have it logged automatically. No dashboards to log into. No end-of-month catch-up sessions. Bookkeeping becomes a passive, always-on activity woven into how you already communicate.

This isn't a replacement for a proper accounting platform yet — you'll still need an MTD-compliant tool for quarterly submissions — but for freelancers who consistently let their bookkeeping slip, AI-first capture tools solve the real problem, which is that the friction of logging expenses is what causes the mess in the first place. Expect this category to grow significantly before the 2027 and 2028 MTD thresholds hit.

If you're choosing your accounting software now, it's worth asking whether your chosen platform has a roadmap for AI-assisted data capture — because within 12–18 months, that feature will likely be table stakes.

How to Choose the Right Tool for Your Freelance Setup

Before you commit to any platform, answer these four questions:

  • Are you already over the MTD threshold? If yes, MTD compatibility isn't optional — make sure your chosen tool is HMRC-recognised for quarterly submissions.
  • Do you invoice international clients? If yes, Xero's multi-currency support puts it ahead of FreeAgent for your use case.
  • Do you bank with NatWest, Mettle, or Tide? If yes, FreeAgent is free. That changes the conversation entirely.
  • Are you keeping costs to an absolute minimum? Pandle Free or Sage's free plan are your best options. Add Pandle Pro at £5/month when you need MTD filing.

It's also worth thinking about your broader business stack. Your accounting software doesn't exist in isolation — it connects to your business bank account, your payment processing setup, and potentially your professional email and business website. Choosing tools that play well together saves hours of manual reconciliation every month.
