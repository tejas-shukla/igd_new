QuickBooks vs FreeAgent UK: Which Should You Choose After the QuickBooks Price Hike?

J
James Okafor
Published July 2026 · 9 min read

If you're weighing up QuickBooks vs FreeAgent UK right now, you're probably doing it for one very specific reason: QuickBooks just got a lot more expensive, and you want to know whether the price is still worth it — or whether FreeAgent is the smarter move for your business in 2026. We've dug into both platforms so you can make the call without spending a weekend on it.

TL;DR — QuickBooks vs FreeAgent UK at a Glance

  • QuickBooks is more powerful for scaling businesses that need multi-currency, CIS, payroll, and deep integrations — but January 2026 price hikes (Plus plan now £50/month, up ~47%) make it hard to justify for micro-businesses and solo founders.
  • FreeAgent is purpose-built for UK tax rules, includes time tracking in every plan, and is completely free if you bank with NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank, or Mettle — making it an easy win for freelancers and limited company directors who want simplicity.
  • Both platforms are HMRC-recognised and MTD-compliant, so whichever you choose, you're covered for Making Tax Digital requirements rolling out from April 2026.

The QuickBooks Price Increase 2026 UK: What Actually Changed?

Let's not dance around it. QuickBooks has become meaningfully more expensive — and if you've been a customer for a few years, the cumulative pain is real. The QuickBooks price increase in 2026 UK pushed the Plus plan from £34 to £50 per month — a 47% jump in a single move. Zoom out to 2022 and you're looking at cumulative price rises of around 75%. For a solo founder or micro-business watching every pound, that's a significant shift.

To be fair, there are cheaper QuickBooks entry points. Simple Start sits at around £15/month, and Essentials at roughly £32/month. But in practice, most small businesses end up on Plus because it's the first tier that includes project tracking and more than three users. Add payroll (approximately £4/month plus £1 per employee) and your real monthly spend lands somewhere between £40 and £55-plus — before any add-ons.

Bottom line: QuickBooks is no longer a budget tool. If you're paying for features you don't use, this is the moment to reassess.

FreeAgent Pricing 2026: The Free Option Most Founders Don't Know About

Here's the thing that surprises a lot of people: FreeAgent is free with a NatWest business account — and also with RBS, Ulster Bank, and Mettle accounts. If you're already banking with one of those, you could switch today and pay nothing. That's not a limited trial or a stripped-back version; it's the full product.

For everyone else, FreeAgent's 2026 pricing sits at approximately £19/month for sole traders and £33/month for limited companies (both ex-VAT), with a 50% introductory discount for the first six months. Compared to QuickBooks Plus at £50/month, that's a meaningful saving — especially if you're a freelancer or a small limited company that doesn't need enterprise-level features.

When you're also thinking about choosing the right business bank account, it's worth factoring in the FreeAgent bundle — the right banking choice could eliminate your accounting bill entirely.

FreeAgent vs QuickBooks Limited Company UK: Which Handles UK Tax Better?

This is where FreeAgent genuinely earns its reputation. In the FreeAgent vs QuickBooks limited company UK comparison, FreeAgent's standout feature is real-time tax visibility. As you record transactions, FreeAgent continuously calculates your Self Assessment liability (for sole traders) or Corporation Tax estimate (for limited companies). No surprises at year-end. No scrambling to find cash in January. It's built for the UK tax system from the ground up — not adapted from a US product.

QuickBooks does handle UK VAT, payroll, and tax well, and it integrates with many accountants' workflows. But its real-time tax estimates aren't as prominently surfaced, and the overall experience can feel more generic — powerful, but not specifically tuned to HMRC's quirks.

FreeAgent also includes time tracking and project management in every plan at no extra cost. If you bill clients by the hour, that matters. QuickBooks requires you to be on Plus or use third-party integrations to get comparable functionality — which adds cost and complexity.

Making Tax Digital Accounting Software UK: Are Both Platforms Ready?

If MTD is on your radar — and from 6 April 2026 it should be — the good news is that both QuickBooks and FreeAgent are HMRC-recognised and fully compliant Making Tax Digital accounting software for UK sole traders and limited companies. MTD for Income Tax (MTD ITSA) becomes mandatory from April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000, dropping to £30,000 from April 2027.

Both platforms can submit quarterly updates directly to HMRC, maintain digital records, and handle the end-of-period statements MTD requires. Neither leaves you exposed. The difference is that FreeAgent's interface is designed to make MTD feel less intimidating — it surfaces what you need to submit and when, without requiring you to understand the underlying HMRC framework. QuickBooks is equally compliant but assumes a bit more user sophistication, or relies on your accountant to manage submissions.

If you're still sorting out your payment processing setup alongside your accounting, it's worth making sure your accounting software connects cleanly with your payment provider — both QuickBooks and FreeAgent offer integrations, but QuickBooks has the edge in breadth here.

Where QuickBooks Still Wins: Multi-Currency, CIS, and Scale

For all the noise around pricing, QuickBooks does have genuine strengths that FreeAgent can't match — and for the right business, they're decisive. The platform supports 157 currencies, making it the obvious choice for founders selling internationally or paying overseas contractors. FreeAgent's multi-currency support is more limited and better suited to primarily UK-based businesses.

QuickBooks also has a built-in CIS (Construction Industry Scheme) module, a stronger mobile app, and a significantly deeper library of third-party integrations. If you're planning to hire a team, integrate with an inventory system, or connect to specialist industry tools, QuickBooks' ecosystem is considerably larger.

A 2024 TrustRadius survey captured this divide clearly: 86% of freelancers preferred FreeAgent, while 78% of small business teams found QuickBooks better for scaling. That's not a rounding error — it reflects a genuine product philosophy difference. QuickBooks is built to grow with you. FreeAgent is built to keep things simple and tax-smart right now.

Cheapest Accounting Software UK Small Business 2026: The Honest Breakdown

If you're hunting for the cheapest accounting software for UK small businesses in 2026, here's the honest picture:

  • Free: FreeAgent via NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank, or Mettle — full product, no catch.
  • ~£9.50/month (first six months): FreeAgent sole trader plan with 50% intro discount.
  • ~£16.50/month (first six months): FreeAgent limited company plan with 50% intro discount.
  • ~£15/month: QuickBooks Simple Start — but with significant feature limitations.
  • ~£32/month: QuickBooks Essentials — better, but still no project tracking.
  • ~£50/month (+payroll): QuickBooks Plus — the plan most small businesses actually need.

If cost is the primary driver and you're a freelancer, sole trader, or small limited company without complex international needs, FreeAgent wins on price at every tier. If you need scale, integrations, and are already working with an accountant who uses QuickBooks, the higher price may still be justified — but go in with eyes open about what you're paying and why.

Don't forget that your business email and communication setup and your website platform all add to your monthly overhead. Saving £20–£35/month on accounting software is real money that compounds across a year.

Which Should You Actually Choose? Our Honest Recommendation

Here's the founder-to-founder version:

  • Choose FreeAgent if you're a freelancer, consultant, contractor, or small limited company director who wants clean UK tax visibility, time tracking included, and either banks with NatWest/RBS/Mettle or wants to save meaningfully on software costs. It's the right tool for the majority of UK micro-businesses.
  • Choose QuickBooks if you're building a team, need multi-currency or CIS support, work with a large accountancy practice already on QuickBooks, or know you'll need deeper integrations as you scale. The higher price buys you genuine capability — just make sure you're actually using it.
  • If you're currently on QuickBooks and feeling the price hike: audit your plan. If you're not using multi-currency, CIS, or payroll, you may be paying £50/month for features a £19/month FreeAgent plan would cover — and cover better for UK tax purposes.
