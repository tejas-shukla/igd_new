Xero vs Wave Accounting Software 2026: Which Is Right for Your Small Business?

J
James Okafor
Published June 2026 · 9 min read

If you're trying to decide between Xero vs Wave accounting software for your small business, you're asking exactly the right question — and the answer isn't as simple as "one is free and one isn't." Both platforms have real strengths, genuine blind spots, and hidden costs that can sneak up on you. This guide cuts through the noise so you can make the call that actually fits your business — not just your current budget.

TL;DR — Xero vs Wave at a Glance

  • Wave wins on price — its Starter plan is permanently free for core accounting and invoicing, and the Pro plan is just $16/month. Xero starts at $25/month with invoice caps.
  • Xero wins on scalability — 1,000+ app integrations, multi-currency support, inventory tracking, and global payroll coverage that Wave simply can't match.
  • Wave's "free" label is misleading — payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.60 per transaction) can easily outpace a flat Xero subscription if you're processing meaningful revenue.

Xero Pricing Plans 2026: What You Actually Pay

Xero offers three US pricing tiers as of June 2026. The Early plan costs $25/month but is capped at 20 invoices and 5 bills per month — a limit that sounds fine until you have a busy quarter. The Growing plan at $55/month removes those caps and gives you unlimited invoices and bills, which is where most growing businesses actually land. The Established plan at $90/month adds multi-currency support and project tracking, making it the tier for businesses operating internationally or running project-based billing.

Here's the catch: those headline numbers are just the starting point. Xero's true monthly cost frequently doubles or triples once you factor in payroll (roughly $45–$60/month base plus $5–$6 per employee in the US), payment processing fees, and the cost of marketplace apps you'll likely need. Realistic all-in costs for a small-to-medium business typically run $50–$200/month on top of the base subscription. That's important to budget for before you commit.

Wave Accounting Pricing 2026: Free, But Read the Fine Print

Wave's best free accounting software for small business positioning is genuinely earned — its Starter plan is permanently free for core accounting, invoicing, and expense tracking. No trial period, no surprise downgrade. The Pro plan at $16/month adds automatic bank imports, receipt scanning, and priority support, making it significantly cheaper than any Xero tier on a subscription basis alone.

But Wave accounting pricing in 2026 comes with a nuance that matters enormously depending on how you get paid. Credit card processing fees run 2.9% + $0.60 per transaction (3.4% + $0.60 for Amex), and bank (ACH) payments cost 1% per transaction with a $1 minimum. Run the numbers: if you're processing $5,000/month in card payments, that's roughly $145–$150 in fees before you've paid for a single software feature. At that point, Xero's $55/month flat fee starts looking like the better deal — especially when Xero also charges payment processing but the base platform cost is already included.

Founder reality check: Wave is genuinely free if you invoice and collect payment outside the platform — bank transfers, checks, or a separate processor. The moment you use Wave Payments, the fee math changes fast.

Wave vs Xero for Freelancers and Solo Founders

For freelancers and solo operators, Wave vs Xero is often a straightforward call: if you're just starting out, have fewer than 10-15 clients, and don't need deep integrations, Wave is an excellent place to begin. The interface is clean, the learning curve is low, and G2 users rate Wave 4.4/5 across 400+ reviews — with ease of use cited consistently as the standout. You can get professional invoicing, track income and expenses, and run basic reports without spending a cent on software.

Xero scores 4.3/5 across 600+ reviews on G2, with praise focused on accounting depth and accountant collaboration — which matters more as your business matures and you need a bookkeeper or CPA to work directly in the platform. If you're working with an accountant, ask which platform they prefer. Many UK, Australian, and Canadian accountants live in Xero; US-based bookkeepers often support both.

If you're still setting up your broader business stack — including your business bank account or payment processing — it's worth thinking through how your accounting software will connect to those choices from day one.

Integrations and Scalability: Where the Gap Gets Serious

This is where the small business bookkeeping software comparison tilts decisively toward Xero for businesses beyond the earliest stage. Xero connects natively with 1,000+ third-party apps — covering everything from inventory management and e-commerce to CRM, payroll, project management, and industry-specific tools. If your operational stack has any complexity, Xero can grow with it.

Wave connects with Shopify, PayPal, Etsy, and Zapier. That's it. Zapier does extend Wave's reach meaningfully, but you're adding another subscription and another layer of complexity to patch gaps that Xero solves natively. For a Shopify store owner who also uses a warehouse management system and a CRM, Wave isn't really in the running.

Wave also has no multi-currency support and no inventory management. If you sell internationally or need to track stock, Wave is a non-starter. Xero's multi-currency lives on the Established plan ($90/month), and basic inventory tracking is included on Growing and above.

Payroll: Neither Platform Makes It Simple

Neither Xero nor Wave includes payroll in their base plans — and both treat it as a paid add-on that materially changes your total cost.

Wave payroll is available only in the US and Canada, starting at $40/month plus $6 per employee. If you're outside those markets, Wave payroll simply doesn't exist for you. Xero payroll covers more global regions and costs roughly $45–$60/month base plus $5–$6 per employee in the US — more expensive, but available where Wave isn't.

If payroll is central to your needs, factor this add-on cost in from the start. A five-person team on Xero's Growing plan with payroll is looking at roughly $55 + $75 = ~$130/month minimum before any other add-ons. On Wave Pro with payroll, it's $16 + $70 = ~$86/month — still cheaper, but narrowing. And that's before you account for Wave's transaction fees on payments.

Xero Alternative Free: Is Wave Really a Substitute?

Searching for a Xero alternative that's free will almost always surface Wave — and for a specific type of business, that framing is fair. If you're a freelancer, a very early-stage founder, or a service business with simple needs and low transaction volume, Wave delivers genuine value at zero cost for the core product.

But "free accounting software" isn't a category — it's a feature of a specific product at a specific moment in your business. Wave is an excellent tool that becomes an expensive workaround as you scale. The businesses that struggle most with Wave are the ones who chose it because it was free, grew into it, and then found themselves duct-taping Zapier workflows to compensate for missing integrations while paying more in transaction fees than they would have on Xero.

The honest answer: Wave is a legitimate starting point, not a permanent solution for most growing businesses. Use it to get off the ground, then revisit when you're processing more than $3,000–$5,000/month in payments or connecting more than two or three external tools. Setting up your business email and website early matters too — and the right accounting software should integrate smoothly with the rest of your stack.

Which Should You Choose? The Decision Framework

Here's the practical breakdown:

  • Choose Wave if: You're pre-revenue or very early stage, you invoice fewer than 15–20 clients per month, you collect payment outside the platform (or have low card volume), you don't need inventory or multi-currency, and you want zero software cost while you validate your business model.
  • Choose Xero if: You're processing meaningful payment volume, you use (or plan to use) more than a handful of third-party tools, you sell internationally or need multi-currency, you want your accountant working directly in the platform, or you're planning to hire employees and need payroll in a non-US/Canada market.
  • Start with Wave, plan to migrate: If you're genuinely bootstrapped and cost is the constraint right now, Wave is a smart starting point — just keep an eye on your transaction fee spend and integration needs. When those start adding friction, the switch to Xero is well-documented and manageable.

One more thing worth thinking about: your accounting software doesn't exist in isolation. It connects to your business bank, your payment processor, and potentially your business domain and website tools. The right choice is the one that fits your whole stack — not just today's budget.
